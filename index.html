Core Foundations of Ethiopian Cuisine

Ethiopian cuisine centres on injera, richly spiced wot stews, and communal eating traditions that emphasise bold flavours and shared plates.

  • Berbere: The signature blend, typically including chilli, garlic, ginger, fenugreek, korarima (Ethiopian cardamom), and warming spices. It is the backbone of many red stews.
  • Mitmita: A hotter, finer blend with African bird's eye chilli, cardamom, and cloves.
  • Niter kibbeh: A clarified butter infused with spices like cardamom, fenugreek, and basil; used to enrich stews and sauté aromatics.

These blends create the cuisine's characteristic deep heat, layered aromatics, and warm spice profile.

Stews and How the Dishes Are Served

1. Injera

Injera is the defining foundation of Ethiopian food. It is usually served with different types of stews, vegetables, lentils, and meat.

  • Made from teff, a tiny, gluten-free grain native to Ethiopia.
  • Fermented for several days, giving it a slightly sour flavour and a spongy texture.
  • Functions as both plate and utensil: dishes are served on top, and diners tear pieces to scoop up stews.
  • Its fermentation and earthy flavour shape the taste of the entire meal.

2. Doro Wat (Chicken)

Doro Wat is a famous Ethiopian chicken stew. It is prepared with onions, berbere spices and traditional ingredients. It is often served with Injera and a boiled egg.

3. Shiro Wat

Shiro is a delicious Ethiopian stew made from ground chickpeas or broad beans. It is mixed with onions, garlic and spices. It is also a popular vegetarian meal.

Why People Love Ethiopian Food?

  • Rich and delicious traditional spices.
  • Fresh and natural ingredients.
  • Many vegetarian and meat options.
  • Unique traditional cooking methods.
  • Beautiful family and sharing traditions.
